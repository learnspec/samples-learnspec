# Music Theory Fundamentals

Learn the building blocks of Western music — notes, scales, intervals, and time signatures — with interactive sheet music examples.

> [!objectives]
> After this lesson, you will be able to:
> - Read notes on the treble clef staff
> - Understand the structure of major and minor scales
> - Identify common time signatures
> - Recognise intervals by name

## Module 1 — Notes and the Staff

### Lesson 1 — The musical staff

Music is written on a **staff** of five horizontal lines. Notes are placed on lines and spaces to indicate pitch.

The **treble clef** (𝄞) is used for higher-pitched instruments and voices. The notes on the lines from bottom to top are **E G B D F** ("Every Good Boy Does Fine"), and the spaces spell **F A C E**.

```abc
X:1
T:Notes on the treble clef lines
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C clef=treble
E G B d|f4|
```

### Lesson 2 — Note durations

Notes have different durations that determine how long they are held:

| Symbol | Name | Beats (in 4/4) |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| 𝅝 | Whole note | 4 |
| 𝅗𝅥 | Half note | 2 |
| ♩ | Quarter note | 1 |
| ♪ | Eighth note | 1/2 |

```abc play
X:1
T:Note durations
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
C4|C2 C2|C C C C|C/2C/2 C/2C/2 C/2C/2 C/2C/2|
w:whole~note half~notes quarter~notes eighth~notes
```

> [!tip]
> Tap your foot on each beat. A quarter note gets one tap, a half note gets two, and a whole note gets four.

## Module 2 — Scales

### Lesson 3 — The major scale

A major scale follows the pattern of **whole** and **half** steps:

**W W H W W W H**

The C major scale uses only natural notes (no sharps or flats):

```abc play cursor
X:1
T:C Major Scale
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:C
CDEF|GABc|
```

> [!note]
> The half steps naturally occur between E–F and B–C on the piano keyboard (no black key between them).

### Lesson 4 — The natural minor scale

The natural minor scale has a different pattern:

**W H W W H W W**

The A natural minor scale also uses only natural notes:

```abc play cursor
X:1
T:A Natural Minor Scale
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:Am
ABCD|EFGa|
```

Compare the two — notice how the minor scale has a darker, more melancholic sound.

```quiz
? The C major scale follows which step pattern?
- [x] W W H W W W H
- [ ] W H W W H W W
- [ ] H W W W H W W
- [ ] W W W H W W H
```

### Lesson 5 — Key signatures

Instead of writing sharps or flats before each note, a **key signature** at the beginning of the staff indicates which notes are altered throughout the piece.

```abc
X:1
T:G Major Scale (one sharp: F#)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:G
GABc|defg|
```

```abc
X:1
T:F Major Scale (one flat: Bb)
M:4/4
L:1/4
K:F
FGAc|Bcdf|
```

> [!tip]
> The order of sharps: **F C G D A E B** ("Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle").
> The order of flats is the reverse: **B E A D G C F**.

## Module 3 — Rhythm and Time

### Lesson 6 — Time signatures

A time signature tells you how beats are grouped in each measure:

| Time signature | Meaning | Feel |
|----------------|---------|------|
| 4/4 | 4 quarter-note beats per measure | Common time (march, pop, rock) |
| 3/4 | 3 quarter-note beats per measure | Waltz feel |
| 6/8 | 6 eighth-note beats per measure | Compound duple (jigs) |

```abc play
X:1
T:3/4 Time — Waltz feel
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:C
C E G|c E G|C E G|c3|
```

```abc play
X:1
T:6/8 Time — Jig feel
M:6/8
L:1/8
K:D
DEF ABc|d3 A3|DEF ABc|d6|
```

```summary
- Music is written on a five-line staff; the treble clef reads higher pitches
- Note durations: whole (4 beats), half (2), quarter (1), eighth (1/2)
- Major scale pattern: W W H W W W H
- Natural minor pattern: W H W W H W W
- Key signatures indicate which notes are sharp or flat
- Time signatures define beat grouping per measure
```
