# Our Solar System

An introduction to our cosmic neighbourhood — the Sun, planets, and the forces that hold it all together.

> [!objectives]
> After this lesson, you will be able to:
> - Name the eight planets in order from the Sun
> - Distinguish between rocky and gas giant planets
> - Explain the role of gravity in orbital mechanics

## Module 1 — The Sun and Inner Planets

### Lesson 1 — The Sun

The Sun is a medium-sized star at the centre of our solar system. It contains 99.86% of the total mass and provides the energy that drives weather, climate, and life on Earth.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Type | G2V main-sequence star |
| Surface temperature | ~5,500 °C |
| Age | ~4.6 billion years |
| Composition | ~73% hydrogen, ~25% helium |

> [!note]
> The Sun fuses about 600 million tonnes of hydrogen into helium every second.

### Lesson 2 — The rocky planets

The four inner planets — Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars — are called **terrestrial** or rocky planets. They share common features:

- Solid surfaces with craters, mountains, or volcanoes
- Relatively small size compared to gas giants
- Few or no moons (Earth has 1, Mars has 2)

### Lesson 3 — Earth's special features

Earth is unique in the solar system for several reasons:

- **Liquid water** covers about 71% of the surface
- A **magnetic field** shields the atmosphere from solar wind
- An **ozone layer** protects life from UV radiation
- A stable **axial tilt** (23.5°) creates seasons

> [!important]
> Earth is the only known planet where liquid water exists on the surface in large quantities — a key ingredient for life as we know it.

## Module 2 — The Outer Solar System

### Lesson 4 — Gas and ice giants

Beyond the asteroid belt lie the four outer planets:

| Planet | Type | Notable feature |
|--------|------|----------------|
| Jupiter | Gas giant | Great Red Spot (persistent storm) |
| Saturn | Gas giant | Spectacular ring system |
| Uranus | Ice giant | Rotates on its side (98° tilt) |
| Neptune | Ice giant | Strongest winds in the solar system |

> [!tip]
> A mnemonic for planet order: **M**y **V**ery **E**xcited **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**achos.

### Lesson 5 — Gravity and orbits

Every object with mass exerts gravitational attraction. Newton's law of universal gravitation:

$$F = G \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^2}$$

Where:
- $F$ is the gravitational force
- $G$ is the gravitational constant ($6.674 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2$)
- $m_1, m_2$ are the masses of the two objects
- $r$ is the distance between their centres

This force keeps planets in elliptical orbits around the Sun, as described by Kepler's laws.

```summary
- The Sun contains 99.86% of the solar system's mass
- Four rocky planets orbit close to the Sun; four gas/ice giants orbit further out
- Earth is unique for its liquid water, magnetic field, and life-supporting atmosphere
- Gravity ($F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$) governs orbital mechanics
```
